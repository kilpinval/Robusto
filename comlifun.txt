How Linux boots
when the kernel can not read your disk yet.
  When Linux starts,the kernel often has no driver for 
your disk yet. The firmware on  motherboard starts
GRUB, and GROUB copies two files from /boot into RAM:
the kernel (vmlinuz) and the initramfs (initrd.img), a small
cpio archive of files.
  The kernel upacks the initramfs into RAM as a temporary root
disk and runs its /init script as PID
1. Debian 13 builds its disk drivers as modules instead of into
the kernel (CONFIG_BLK_DEV_NVME=m), so one generic kernel boots every
PC; each PC needs only one of them, and Debian packs most of those
modules into the initramfs
(MODULES=most); the initramfs carries those modules (nvme.ko.xz among them).
The script starts udev (line 222, run_scripts /scripts/init-top,
udev loads the NVMe driver into the kernel, and the script mounts your
real disk and switches the root to it (Debian is init ends with exec 
run-init; other distros use switch_root(8). GNU GROUB
